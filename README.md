# Improved Zetamac

A browser drill based on the layout and controls of [Arithmetic Zetamac](https://arithmetic.zetamac.com/), with quality of life improvements and more ways to practice. The goal is to keep the drill as simple to start and play as the original.

[Play on GitHub Pages](https://simonvu617.github.io/Improved-Zetamac/)

Other Zetamac tools give you more ways to track your progress. [ZetaLog](https://www.zetalog.co.uk/how-it-works) saves your scores and offers sign-in for syncing and leaderboards. [R3CK0N](https://r3ck0n.com/?play=math) times each problem and adds daily challenges. [Aiden Seo’s Zetamac](https://aidenseo.com/zetamac/) lets you drill your slowest problems and play with friends in multiplayer rooms. Those features can help you analyze your practice or compete, but they add more to navigate. The appeal of Zetamac is how little stands between you and a round. Improved Zetamac keeps that flow: no login, no dashboard. Pick your settings and start.

## Quality of life

- **Space to start or restart.** Press Space to start from settings or to begin a fresh round with the same settings during or after a game. When your fingers are on the number row, your thumb is already near the space bar.
- **Dark mode.** Light numbers stay clear against a dark background, with less glare than Zetamac’s white page in a dark room.
- **Saved settings.** The game remembers the settings from your last round in the same browser so you can come back and start right away.
- **Esc and reset.** Press Esc during or after a round to return to settings. **Reset to defaults** restores the original ranges, operations, and 120-second timer.
- **A steady answer box.** The box stays in place as problems change. A correct answer moves you to the next problem as you type.

## Added features

- **Decimals.** Set the minimum and maximum number of decimal places and mix decimal problems into a round.
- **Fractions.** Choose separate ranges for numerators and denominators. Fractions are stacked in the problem, and you can type answers with a slash. Equivalent answers like `2/4` and `1/2` both count.
- **Powers.** Set a base range and an exponent range. Use an exponent of 2 for squares, or set a range to mix in higher powers.
- **Roots.** Reverse the powers problems using the same base and exponent ranges.
